# Muslingo Beginner Islamic Studies Curriculum Research

This report evaluates current beginner Islamic-studies curricula and official video-course libraries for possible use as Muslingo references, partners, or learner referrals. It was checked on 10 September 2026. Prices and availability can change. “Trustworthy” here means publicly evidenced scholarly responsibility, declared doctrinal scope, identifiable source practice, educational structure, and a corrections/review process—not a claim that every Muslim community will accept every theological or legal choice.

## Executive conclusion

No reviewed provider simultaneously offers all of the following: complete coverage of the requested topics, age-banded app pedagogy, transparent support for multiple Sunni legal/theological traditions, strong assessment, granular source provenance, localization rights, and a commercial adaptation licence.

Muslingo should therefore build an original, reviewed curriculum rather than digitize or paraphrase one publisher’s books. The best reference stack is:

1. **Ages 5–11 foundation:** Safar Publications as the strongest all-round printed benchmark for sequencing, teacher support, named editorial contributors, references, and classroom testing. Its fiqh is Hanafi and its content is not reusable without written permission.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
2. **Ages 11–18 contemporary supplement:** Yaqeen Curriculum for discussion-based faith, ethics, identity, and difficult modern questions. It explicitly describes itself as a supplement, not a complete foundational curriculum.[^15]
3. **Teen/adult structured video pathway:** SeekersGuidance where Muslingo wants traditional Sunni instruction with explicit Hanafi/Shafi‘i pathways, Ash‘ari/Maturidi theology, classical texts, quizzes, examinations, oral testing, and named instructors.[^18][^19]
4. **Optional Athari/Salafi pathway:** Zad Academy for a free, assessed, two-year, seven-subject program for ages 15+. Its “Qur’an and Sunnah according to the understanding of the pious predecessors” framing is explicit and should be labelled, not blended invisibly into another theological track.[^22][^23]
5. **Qur’an context and Arabic:** Bayyinah TV is the strongest consumer-video benchmark for accessible, self-paced Qur’an journeys, but it is specialist rather than comprehensive and prohibits reproduction or modification without permission.[^30][^31]
6. **Seerah and seasonal learning:** Qalam’s official Seerah podcast and Ramadan/Hajj recordings are useful, teacher-led supplements. Qalam invites people to share the Seerah recordings, while its general terms still prohibit derivative or commercial exploitation without permission.[^32][^33][^34]
7. **Hadith literacy:** SeekersGuidance’s free beginner introduction to hadith terminology is the clearest reviewed entry point because it teaches how reports are categorized and evaluated, not merely a list of sayings.[^20]

Safar, An Nasihah, Weekend Learning, and International Curricula (ICO) are valuable printed benchmarks; none presents an open commercial adaptation licence. Yaqeen, SeekersGuidance, Zad, AMAU, Bayyinah, and Qalam are valuable digital or video benchmarks; “free to learn” or “free to share” does not mean “free to copy into a paid app.”

## Recommended Muslingo architecture

### Core pathway

Build three age bands with the same recurring strands:

- **Foundation, roughly 7–10:** Allah and the pillars of faith; cleanliness, prayer, fasting; short seerah episodes; prophetic stories; everyday manners; Qur’an meaning and source habits.
- **Bridge, roughly 11–14:** reasons for belief; puberty-aware worship; seerah chronology and moral choices; hadith provenance basics; peer/family ethics; Ramadan and Hajj as integrated journeys.
- **Independent learner, 15+/adult:** a declared aqidah track, a declared madhhab track, comparative literacy without ruling-shopping, Qur’an context, hadith method, full seerah, ethics, and applied worship.

Every strand should spiral: encounter a concept, practise it, revisit it in a new scenario, then explain or apply it. A user should never encounter an unlabelled mixture of Hanafi, Shafi‘i, Maliki, Hanbali, Ash‘ari, Maturidi, or Athari positions.

### Editorial model

Use a minimum three-role sign-off for every lesson:

1. **Subject scholar:** verifies doctrine, law, tafsir, hadith, or history.
2. **Learning editor:** verifies age suitability, objective, interaction, and assessment.
3. **Source editor:** verifies Qur’an references, hadith locator and grading, quotation accuracy, permissions, and translation provenance.

For legal rulings, the lesson metadata should identify the school followed and record significant, learner-relevant variation. For creed, the product should publish a one-page scope statement and label any optional track. For seerah and prophetic stories, distinguish Qur’anic report, sound hadith, early historical report, and later devotional narration.

## Topic-by-topic recommendation

### Aqidah

**Best benchmark:** SeekersGuidance for a declared Ash‘ari/Maturidi traditional Sunni pathway; Zad or AMAU only for a clearly labelled Athari/Salafi pathway. Yaqeen is especially strong for teen questions about God, prophethood, revelation, suffering, and decree, but its units are supplementary rather than a complete creed sequence.[^15][^19][^22]

**Muslingo requirement:** publish the doctrinal scope before writing lessons. Start with agreed pillars of faith, worship of Allah, prophethood, revelation, the afterlife, and divine decree. Put school-specific formulations in named tracks or scholar notes; do not disguise one as “neutral.”

### Worship

**Best benchmark:** Safar for young learners; SeekersGuidance for adult Hanafi/Shafi‘i sequence; Zad for a comprehensive single-path adult course. Seekers’ *On Worship* covers purification, prayer, fasting, zakat, and Hajj through a Hanafi primer and explicitly points learners to a Shafi‘i alternative.[^21]

**Muslingo requirement:** interactive worship lessons must be school-specific and reviewed by a qualified jurist. Assessment should include ordered-action tasks, scenario judgment, and “ask a scholar” boundaries—not only multiple choice.

### Seerah

**Best benchmark:** Qalam’s full official Seerah podcast for narrative and chronology, with Safar/An Nasihah for age-banding. Qalam explicitly frames its series as classical-source material for English speakers and permits sharing of the recordings.[^32]

**Muslingo requirement:** maintain a chronology and evidence tag for each episode. Separate well-established events from disputed details and later devotional storytelling.

### Qur’an context

**Best benchmark:** Bayyinah TV for consumer-friendly journeys; SeekersGuidance for structured Qur’anic studies and classical-text context; Yaqeen for teen worldview and research-rich topical units.[^18][^30]

**Muslingo requirement:** every passage lesson needs surah/ayah, translation edition, Makki/Madani status where relevant, immediate textual context, cautious use of asbab al-nuzul, and a distinction between tafsir and personal reflection.

### Hadith literacy

**Best benchmark:** SeekersGuidance’s free beginner *Introduction to the Science of Hadith: Bayquni’s Hadith Terminology*. It explicitly teaches categories, authenticity questions, and the critical method used by hadith scholars.[^20]

**Muslingo requirement:** teach “what a hadith citation means” before gamifying isolated sayings. Show collection, locator/number, grading and grader where relevant, translation source, and whether a juristic conclusion depends on broader evidence. Avoid declaring a ruling from one decontextualized report.

### Ethics and adab

**Best benchmark:** Yaqeen for facilitated teen ethical reasoning; An Nasihah and Safar for habits and age progression; Qalam’s children’s recordings for accessible delivery.[^1][^6][^15][^33]

**Muslingo requirement:** use realistic scenarios, reflection, behaviour planning, and follow-up rather than points for memorizing moral vocabulary. Make privacy, bullying, digital conduct, family duties, neighbours, care for creation, and disagreement etiquette explicit.

### Prophetic stories

**Best benchmark:** An Nasihah/Safar for integrated progression; Qalam “Stories from the Qur’an for Kids”; Bayyinah Story Nights for narrative craft.[^1][^6][^30][^33]

**Muslingo requirement:** label the source layer. Qur’anic details should anchor the lesson; sound hadith can extend it; Isra’iliyyat and popular embellishments need explicit review or omission.

### Ramadan

**Best benchmark:** Qalam’s Ramadan resource hub and SeekersGuidance’s Ramadan Curriculum. Qalam organizes free webinars, fasting/zakat fiqh, age-specific classes, Qur’an connection, and reflection series; Seekers offers daily English/Arabic scholar-led classes.[^18][^33]

**Muslingo requirement:** a four-part seasonal path—prepare, practise, troubleshoot, sustain—covering intention, fasting rules by selected madhhab, Qur’an, character, zakat al-fitr/Eid, exemptions, and post-Ramadan habits.

### Hajj

**Best benchmark:** SeekersGuidance *On Worship*, Zad’s third-semester fasting/Hajj/Umrah module, and Qalam’s Hajj/Umrah recordings.[^21][^22][^33]

**Muslingo requirement:** separate spiritual meaning from step-by-step legal performance. Provide a visual sequence, terminology, place map, common mistakes, and a prominent warning that real pilgrims should follow their group’s qualified scholar and current travel/health authorities.

## Prioritized gaps for Muslingo

### P0 — Resolve before content production

1. **Governance charter.** Name an accountable scholarly board, learning-design lead, source editor, correction route, review dates, and conflict-of-interest policy. Most commercial curricula disclose some scholars but few disclose the complete operating process.
2. **Doctrinal and madhhab scope.** Decide whether Muslingo is (a) one named tradition, (b) a common-core product with separate legal/theological tracks, or (c) a comparative literacy product for older learners. Option (b) is the safest broad-audience design; it requires separate juristic review for each track.
3. **Rights and licensing.** None of the shortlisted comprehensive curricula grants a clear commercial adaptation licence. Use them for evaluation only. Create original lesson wording and activities from authorized primary/secondary sources, or negotiate a written licence covering app use, translation, audio/video, quizzes, territories, term, updates, and derivative works.
4. **Lesson-level provenance.** Store citations as structured data: source type, Arabic text where licensed, translation, locator, grading, scholar/reviewer, madhhab/aqidah tag, permission status, version, and last review date.
5. **Age and prerequisite map.** “Beginner” is not an age. Define reading load, abstract reasoning, puberty-related content, prior worship knowledge, and Arabic familiarity for each course. An Nasihah’s public age mapping ambiguity is a useful warning.
6. **Correction/version system.** Religious content requires visible corrections, rollback, and learner notification. Yaqeen’s public commitment to update or archive content is the strongest benchmark.[^16]

### P1 — Highest curriculum opportunities

7. **Hadith literacy before hadith memorization.** Children’s curricula often teach selected ahadith but rarely teach provenance, grading, commentary, and limits of lay inference. A short, age-banded “How we know this report” strand would differentiate Muslingo.
8. **Qur’an context with guardrails.** Avoid reducing tafsir to motivational reflections. Separate translation, tafsir, occasion reports, linguistic notes, and application.
9. **Worship practice assessment.** Most digital providers rely heavily on quizzes. Add drag-to-sequence, branching scenarios, self-check practice, and teacher/parent observation—without pretending a device can certify ritual validity.
10. **Seerah evidence confidence.** Give each episode a source-status tag and avoid popular but weak details unless clearly marked and pedagogically justified.
11. **Seasonal continuity.** Ramadan and Hajj should not be temporary content islands. Link them to aqidah, worship, Qur’an, ethics, seerah, and post-season habits.
12. **Localization with fresh scholarly review.** Translation changes legal vocabulary and sometimes implied doctrine. Each language needs a glossary, named translator, juristic reviewer, style guide, and audio pronunciation review. Do not assume an English-approved lesson remains approved after localization.

### P2 — Product and partnership opportunities

13. **Licensing-first partnerships.** Prioritize exploratory conversations with Safar (children’s core), Yaqeen (teen units), SeekersGuidance (adult/hadith/worship), and Qalam (seerah/seasonal audio). Ask for a pilot licence for a small module before discussing a full catalogue.
14. **Source-facing learner UI.** Make citations tappable but age-appropriate: a simple “Where this comes from” card for children and a full source drawer for teens/adults.
15. **Scholar escalation.** Add a clear boundary between education and fatwa. Route personal or unusual worship questions to a qualified local scholar or an approved partner; do not generate personalized rulings from generic course text.
16. **Outcome evidence.** Pilot comprehension, retention, application, and error rates by age group. Treat completion streaks as engagement data, not proof of religious understanding.

## Partnership diligence checklist

Before signing with any provider, request:

- exact doctrine and madhhab statement for the licensed material;
- names and qualifications of current writers, reviewers, and final approvers;
- sample source apparatus and hadith-grading policy;
- correction history and turnaround process;
- age-validation and classroom/user testing evidence;
- full content inventory and prerequisite map;
- commercial mobile-app, web, audio, video, quiz, and translation rights;
- right to edit for length and accessibility, with approval workflow;
- territories, languages, exclusivity, duration, renewal, and takedown rules;
- ownership of Muslingo-original interactions and learner data;
- accessibility files, transcripts, image rights, and voice/talent releases;
- source files and update delivery format;
- indemnity and process for disputed religious or copyright claims.

## Recommended first pilot

Build one original 12-lesson “Prayer Foundations” pilot for ages 11–14:

- 2 lessons on why Muslims worship and the place of prayer;
- 3 on purification, with separate Hanafi and Shafi‘i branches;
- 4 on the prayer sequence, meanings, and common mistakes;
- 1 on congregation and mosque manners;
- 1 on missed/forgotten steps and the boundary for asking a scholar;
- 1 cumulative scenario assessment and a non-certifying practice checklist.

Use Safar only as a sequencing/age benchmark, SeekersGuidance as a declared-madhhab teaching benchmark, and Yaqeen as a discussion/meaning benchmark. Draft every word and interaction originally, cite approved sources, and put the complete pilot through scholar, learning-design, source, child-safeguarding, and licensing review. This small module will expose the hardest issues—madhhab branching, source metadata, interaction design, correction flow, and localization—before Muslingo commits to a full library.
