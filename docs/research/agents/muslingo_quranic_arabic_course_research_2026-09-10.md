# Structured Quranic Arabic and Arabic Literacy Courses for Muslingo

## Executive summary

Muslingo does not need more undifferentiated lesson volume. Its current repository already contains 246 lessons: 100 Arabic-literacy lessons, 100 Quran lessons, 36 Tajwid lessons, and 10 Islamic-foundations lessons. The product is strongest in letter recognition, reading mechanics, recitation practice, Quran audio, short-surah exposure, and varied micro-interactions. Its central curriculum gap is the bridge from “I can decode or repeat this text” to “I can independently analyze and understand a new Quranic phrase.”

The strongest current competitors solve different pieces of that bridge:

- Bayyinah Dream provides the clearest full academic sequence: beginner nahw, beginner sarf, intermediate grammar/morphology/readers, then balaghah and advanced texts.
- Quranic and Understand Al Quran use frequency-first vocabulary and immediate application to Quran and salah.
- Arabic101 combines a coursebook, a companion app, a workbook, contextual Quran examples, pronunciation, and explicit self-assessment.
- AlifBee offers the most complete mainstream app pattern: placement, ten progressive MSA levels, four-skill practice, stories, spaced review, and certificates.
- SeekersGuidance demonstrates a useful dual-track architecture: living Arabic plus a separate technical track for nahw, sarf, and balaghah.
- Coursera and OpenLearn show how a short alphabet/phonology course can still include explicit learning outcomes, graded assignments or quizzes, writing practice, and completion evidence.

The recommended Muslingo priority is therefore a new, original “Understand Quranic Arabic” spine with prerequisites and cumulative mastery gates. It should be separate from, but connected to, the existing Arabic Reading, Tajwid, and Quran/surah tracks. The first release should cover script diagnostics, 250–500 high-frequency Quranic lemmas, core particles and pronouns, nominal and verbal sentence patterns, introductory morphology, word-by-word and phrase-level parsing, and short unseen-ayah comprehension. A legal/content audit is release-critical: free-to-view courses and YouTube videos are usually copyrighted and are not a reusable content library.

## Scope and evidence standard

Claims about curriculum, price, languages, assessment, and licensing are based on official product, university, store, support, or terms pages. “Not publicly confirmed” means the official public pages reviewed did not establish the feature; it does not prove the feature is absent behind login. Prices can vary by country, app store, promotion, and tax. Licensing labels are product-planning guidance, not legal advice.

## Patterns worth adopting with original content

### 1. Separate the routes, then reconnect them

The best programs do not force every learner through the same path. Muslingo should present four connected routes:

1. **Read Arabic** — script, decoding, writing, phoneme discrimination, fluent vowelled reading.
2. **Recite Quran** — makharij, sifat, Tajwid rules, guided recitation, human-review option.
3. **Understand Quranic Arabic** — frequency vocabulary, nahw, sarf, phrase parsing and unseen-ayah comprehension.
4. **Memorize and reflect** — listening, chunking, recall, meaning, and safe contextual reflection with cited scholarship.

Placement should allow a learner who can already read the mushaf to skip basic script lessons but should not let a reader bypass phonological or comprehension diagnostics automatically.

### 2. Use a frequency-first vocabulary spine, not isolated word lists

Quranic and Understand Al Quran demonstrate the motivational value of early coverage. Muslingo should build an independently computed frequency list with explicit methodology: normalize orthographic variants, distinguish surface forms from lemmas, preserve particles, identify roots cautiously, and calculate coverage by token count. Each item should be learned in three layers:

- recognition of the surface form;
- lemma/root and relevant inflection;
- meaning in a real ayah and contrast with at least one other context.

Coverage claims should say “percentage of word occurrences recognized,” not “percentage of the Quran understood.” Vocabulary frequency alone does not establish sentence meaning.

### 3. Teach grammar as a comprehension tool

Bayyinah, Arabic101 and SeekersGuidance make grammar valuable by applying it to authentic text. Every Muslingo grammar lesson should follow one stable loop:

1. notice a pattern in a short Quranic phrase;
2. receive a plain-language explanation;
3. classify or manipulate the pattern;
4. reconstruct the phrase;
5. explain how the structure changes meaning;
6. apply it to a new phrase or ayah;
7. retrieve it again after a delay.

The aim is not merely naming terms such as *ism*, *fi‘l*, *harf*, *mudaf*, or *maf‘ul*. The learner should show that the term helps recover meaning.

### 4. Pair app practice with durable assessment

Arabic101’s coursebook → app → workbook loop and AlifBee’s placement → lessons → spaced review → certificate path both solve a weakness of pure micro-learning: completing a screen can be mistaken for durable learning. Muslingo should keep its current game-like steps but add unit tests, delayed review, and unseen transfer tasks.

### 5. Distinguish machine feedback from qualified recitation review

Muslingo already records speech and can call a remote evaluator, with a deterministic local fallback when the backend is unavailable. That is useful practice infrastructure, but it is not equivalent to Tajwid certification. Product copy and lesson results should separate:

- device/transcript match;
- phoneme-duration or acoustic similarity;
- rule-specific recitation evidence;
- qualified human correction.

Only the last category should support strong claims about correct Tajwid or ijazah-style mastery.

## Muslingo current-state curriculum audit

The audit used the current local checkout of `baimukhanalan/musingo` on `main`, commit `1532bf7`.

### Existing strengths

- **Scale:** 100 Arabic, 100 Quran, 36 Tajwid and 10 foundations lessons.
- **Arabic literacy:** all 28 letters, short and long vowels, sukun, tanwin, shadda, madd, connected forms, non-connecting letters, sun/moon letters, stopping, semantic chunking, and repeated contrast practice.
- **Tajwid:** makharij zones, individual articulation families, sifat, qalqalah, ghunnah, nun/tanwin and mim rules, multiple madd types, tafkhim/tarqiq, and waqf signs.
- **Quran practice:** real ayah audio, translation/meaning questions, word ordering, matching, speech attempts, juz/surah coverage and strengthened mastery copies.
- **Interaction variety:** audio, text, multiple choice, matching, speaking, word order, and listen-choice steps.
- **Product foundations:** progress, XP, streaks, lesson availability, Quran reader and daily ayah.

### Structural weaknesses

- The Arabic course is overwhelmingly a reading/pronunciation course. Only a small number of lessons introduce roots, form and meaning; there is no full public-facing Quranic nahw/sarf spine comparable to Bayyinah, Arabic101 or SeekersGuidance.
- The Quran course exposes many verses and translations but does not systematically teach the linguistic tools needed to analyze a new verse independently.
- There is no writing-step type for tracing, connected-form production, copying, keyboard composition or dictation.
- Course objects have a simple order and completion state, but no explicit competency prerequisites, placement bands, mastery thresholds by skill, or cumulative certification map.
- The UI supports Russian, Kazakh and English, but lesson titles, questions and explanatory fields are predominantly authored in Russian. A localized shell is not yet a trilingual curriculum.
- Source references are plain strings/URLs, not a rights-aware content manifest with author, edition, license, permitted use, reviewer and verification date.
- Pronunciation scoring can fall back locally. That keeps the lesson usable, but a fallback similarity score must not be presented as proof of correct makhraj or Tajwid.

## Recommended original Muslingo sequence

### Stage 0 — placement

- Script and diacritics recognition
- Decode five unfamiliar vowelled words
- Eight phoneme-contrast items
- Ten high-frequency Quranic words
- Five structure questions
- One short recitation sample, clearly labeled practice rather than certification

### Stage 1 — Arabic literacy and sound

1. Letter families by shape and dot pattern
2. Isolated, initial, medial and final forms
3. Non-connecting letters
4. Short vowels and sukun
5. Long vowels and basic madd timing
6. Shadda and tanwin
7. Hamzah variants, ta marbuta and alif maqsura
8. Sun/moon letters and common Quran symbols
9. Minimal phoneme contrasts and makhraj families
10. Word reading, dictation and connected-form writing

Existing Muslingo lessons can be mapped into this stage, but actual writing steps and mastery gates must be added.

### Stage 2 — Quranic vocabulary and sentence foundations

1. The first 25 high-frequency particles/pronouns
2. Ism, fi‘l and harf as meaning tools
3. Attached pronouns
4. Prepositional phrases
5. Definite/indefinite and adjective agreement
6. Idafa
7. Nominal sentences
8. Perfect-tense verbal sentences
9. Negation, questions and connectors
10. Cumulative 100-word unseen-phrase assessment

### Stage 3 — morphology and expanding comprehension

1. Roots and pattern recognition
2. Perfect and imperfect conjugation
3. Imperative and prohibition
4. Form I families
5. High-frequency derived forms, introduced by semantic function
6. Active/passive participles and verbal nouns
7. Weak, hamzated and doubled roots
8. 250-word milestone
9. Short-ayah parsing without transliteration
10. Multi-ayah comprehension capstone

### Stage 4 — independent Quran reading

- Expand to 500+ lemmas
- Surah-based units that reactivate prior grammar and morphology
- Unseen ayah and passage questions
- Gradual removal of word-level glosses
- Optional classical-reader and tafsir bridge
- Human-reviewed oral recitation pathway kept separate from language comprehension certification

## Product decisions

1. **Do not merge Tajwid and Arabic-language scoring.** A learner may understand a phrase but recite it poorly, or recite accurately without understanding it. Track both.
2. **Do not organize the comprehension curriculum only by surah order.** Organize the skill spine by linguistic dependency, then select ayat that demonstrate each skill.
3. **Do not treat transliteration as a permanent scaffold.** Use it briefly, measure dependence, and fade it before script fluency is certified.
4. **Do not promise “80% understanding” from frequency alone.** Publish transparent token-coverage metrics and separate lexical recognition from comprehension.
5. **Do not import competitor worksheets, recordings, screenshots, explanations, or question wording.** Benchmark the learning architecture, then author and review original content.
6. **Make Kazakh a real course language.** The current regional advantage is weakened if the lesson content remains Russian while only interface chrome changes.

## Partnership and licensing shortlist

If Muslingo wants licensed rather than fully original curriculum components, the first outreach candidates are:

1. **Understand Al Quran Academy** — strongest multilingual and school implementation fit; request commercial digital adaptation rights, not merely download permission.
2. **Arabic101** — strongest modular literacy/Tajwid/Quranic-Arabic asset fit and explicit academy infrastructure; negotiate app adaptation, localization, audio and assessment rights separately.
3. **Quranic** — strongest frequency-first product overlap and an existing school/organization offer; explore B2B access or a co-branded vocabulary layer.
4. **AlifBee** — strongest mainstream MSA/localization and school-license infrastructure; explore placement, writing or institutional technology partnership, not content scraping.
5. **Bayyinah** — strongest advanced Quranic grammar pathway, but public terms are restrictive; a formal partnership is required for any content use.

LQToronto is valuable for study and sequencing research but explicitly bars commercial use. OpenLearn, Coursera, Busuu, Duolingo, AMAU and SeekersGuidance should be treated as architecture benchmarks unless their owners provide a separate written license.
